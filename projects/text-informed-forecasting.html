Game revenue forecasting with two foundation models

An M.Sc. thesis on free-to-play game revenue: Forecasting Free-to-Play Game Revenue with Foundation Models and Agent-Compiled Text Covariates.

LLM agentsTabPFN-TSChronos-2Time series

The forecasting problem

Each game's daily net revenue is forecast as two components, daily active users and revenue per daily active user, multiplied day by day over a 90-day horizon. The forecasts cover three live free-to-play strategy games. Historical observations and calendar features are the numerical base. Planning documents supply further context: the company wiki, official forums, and public game wikis, read only in the versions that existed at each forecast origin.

My thesis studies this setup as a company project at InnoGames. The questions are whether agent-compiled covariates from the planning documents are informative, and how the two frozen models compare with the references below.

From documents to forecasts

An LLM agent compiles covariates from those planning documents into a fixed schema. The agent is a local open-weight model run through Ollama. A second open-weight model is used to check whether results depend on the backbone. The compiled fields follow that fixed schema and are the covariates passed to the forecasters.

The documents are read only as they stood at the forecast origin. A later edit can mention something that was not known when the forecast would have been made, so the version available on that date is the one that enters the schema.

Two frozen time-series foundation models produce the forecasts, with calendar features and no task-specific training. TabPFN-TS uses the TabPFN-3.5 checkpoint. Chronos-2 is the second engine. When the text route is included, the agent-compiled covariates go in with the history and the calendar features.

Testing the forecasts

The evaluation grid has 54 monthly forecast origins, from 2022 to 2026. Each origin uses only observations and document versions available on that date. A held-out block is opened once, after the protocol is frozen. A pre-registered protocol with a frozen hash governs that look. Experiments are running, and the held-out evaluation is still pending.

The engines are compared with the company's production forecast, the analysts' own delivered plan, and classical references: LightGBM, ARIMA, ETS, and seasonal naive. The text route is tested against a shuffled-text placebo.

Daily accuracy uses MASE, with a weekly seasonal period, and MAPE. A plan-to-date error is used at the quarter level. The evaluation also checks prediction intervals, and it includes a selection-aware test for the choice between the two models.

My contribution

I developed the workflow from the planning documents and the LLM agent through the two frozen forecasters to the evaluation, including the placebo and the reference comparisons.

The project is my M.Sc. thesis in Management & Data Science at Leuphana University. Experiments are running, the held-out evaluation is pending, and I am writing the thesis for submission in January 2027. This overview covers the approach; company data and research results are not published here.
